# Ideas: a long list, criticised, and the rounds that build them

The rule for keeping an idea: it must put a *decision* in front of the player that a person of 1618–1630 actually faced, or show the
world moving without them in a way they can see. "Flavour only" ideas are kept only if they cost a few lines. Each idea has a round
(R1–R10) or a reason it was cut.

## Critique of what exists (before adding)
- The open world has a lot of verbs but few *goals*: nothing tells you what a good life looks like, and nothing sums one up at the end. → epitaph, life goals (R1, R8).
- Money has few sinks after the first year: a smith's son with 5,000 wen has nothing to want. → land, a shop, horse, family upkeep, bribes (R1, R5).
- Families are static furniture; they should need you, age and die. → R3.
- The army path is two battles and a promotion. → R2.
- Exams are three multiple-choice questions; the real ordeal (nine days in a cell, the eight-legged form, the gate search) is missing. → R1.
- After Liaoyang falls, Liaodong is closed. Life under the Jin was the fate of most Liaodong people. → R4.
- Office is cases at random; no factions you can feel until the purge. → R7.
- Death ends everything; no legacy. → R8.

## R1 (done, v164): festivals, trade, the examination hall, history's notables
1. Spring Festival (1/1): the family meal, red couplets, if you are away a line in the journal; money to parents (filial).
2. Lantern Festival (1/15): lantern riddles in town (a learning test with a prize).
3. Qingming (3/5): sweep the graves of the dead (family you lost); not going costs filial standing.
4. Dragon Boat (5/5): zongzi, realgar wine; in Beijing the boat race crowd (pickpocket risk).
5. Mid-Autumn (8/15): mooncakes; a homesick night if away from home.
6. Double Ninth (9/9): climb a height; the old remembered.
7. Winter solstice (11/15 in this calendar): dumplings; the start of the hard months.
8. A trade spread you can see: ginseng cheap in Fushun, dear in Beijing; cloth the other way; salt dear in Liaodong.
9. Travel goods weigh: a pack holds 10 items; a horse 30; the road costs more with a horse (fodder) but takes 2/3 the days.
10. A horse: buy from the Jurchen trader at Fushun (cheap) or in Beijing (dear). Sell it when you need money.
11. Hire a carter: a seat on a cart for a long road, cheaper than a horse, not faster.
12. Smuggling after Fushun falls: iron to the Jin is a capital crime; huge profit; patrols search packs.
13. The Seven Grievances (七大恨, 1618/4/13) posted copies circulate: read one at the notice board.
14. The gate search (搜檢) at the exam hall: candidates are stripped; a crib-shirt (夾帶) can be bought and may be found (expelled, cangue).
15. Nights in the cell: three random cell events (rain through the roof, a neighbour taken ill, your candle runs low) with choices that cost score or health.
16. The eight-legged form: one paper question asks you to put the eight parts in order (破題 承題 起講 入題 起股 中股 後股 束股).
17. Book learning: model essays raise the score cap on the hard paper; the Four Books with Zhu Xi's commentary help the first paper.
18. The licentiate's yearly test (歲考): fall too far behind in learning and you are demoted (or flogged).
19. Licentiate privileges, real: exempt from corvée, cannot be beaten by a magistrate without first being stripped of the degree; folk call you 相公.
20. Xu Guangqi in Beijing: astronomy, Euclid (his translation, 1607), the red barbarian cannon; learning goes up, a "Western learning" flag, and in 1619 an offer to train at Tongzhou.
21. Yuan Chonghuan is a candidate at the 1619 metropolitan exam (historically he passed): meet him at the lodging house; if befriended, a letter later.
22. Epitaph (墓誌): when a life ends, a page sums it up: the offices, the children, the dead you buried, what you did on the hard days.

## R2 (done, v165): the army
23. Guard duty at night: a choice when a shadow moves (shout, shoot, go look).
24. Rations sold by the officers: report it (the officer hates you), steal back, or say nothing.
25. Pay that doesn't come after Sarhu: three months in arrears; the company talks mutiny.
26. Desertion: walk away; you are a deserter (soldiers in Liaoyang may recognise you, a flogging or worse).
27. Heads taken (首級): kills in a world battle become a count; heads buy rank (the real Ming reward).
28. Your company: five generated comrades with names; they die in battles and the journal remembers.
29. Household retainer (家丁): at arms ≥ 40 a general's man offers you a place among his retainers: double pay, better armour.
30. Weapons you made go into battle with you (a sword you forged).
31. The Hun River battle (1621/3, map toba) if you are in the army in Shenyang's spring.
32. Mao Wenlong recruits daring men for the coast (1621/5): go, and the East River campaign opens as a continuation.
33. Xiong Tingbi's inspection (1619/8): drilled men rewarded, the rest beaten.
34. Garrison at the Pass after 1621 (Shanhai): a soldier's life on the wall.

## R3 (done, v166): family
35. Mother ages; she needs money each month (a letter with silver: filial standing).
36. Erniu grows: at 15 she marries (the Suns if betrothed, or a match you arrange); her dowry is yours to find.
37. Your children grow: a son can be sent to school (cost per year), a daughter's feet bound or not (a real, hard choice).
38. Father teaches: working the forge with him raises smithing faster than alone; his death leaves you his hammer.
39. Mourning (丁憂): a parent's death forces an official to resign for 27 months (real law); hiding it is a crime.
40. Filial standing (孝) as a number: it counts in evaluations and with matchmakers.
41. Your wife has a name and a temper (generated); she can run the house's money (a small income) or not.
42. Childbirth can kill (a real risk): a physician helps.
43. A widow remarries or keeps chaste (a memorial arch for chaste widows).

## R4 (done, v167): under the Jin
44. If you stay in Liaodong in 1621: shave the head (薙髮) or hide.
45. Han households assigned to Jurchen households (編莊, 1621–23): a Jurchen master takes half the grain.
46. The 1623 grain-hoarder purge: the Jin kill Han who hide grain.
47. Escape to Mao Wenlong at Zhenjiang (1621/7) — the road east to the coast.
48. Serve the Jin: Li Yongfang's Han soldiers; rank among the Jin.
49. The Jin examination of 1629 (Hong Taiji tested Han scholars; the passed were freed from bondage): real.
50. A Jurchen neighbour: generated folk of the Jin, trade and friction.

## R5 (done, v168): money and property
51. Land: buy mu in the village (price by year, falls in war); autumn rent; tax per mu including the Liaodong levy.
52. Pawnshop: pawn goods for 50%, redeem within 3 months.
53. Moneylender: borrow at 3% a month; collectors come.
54. Rent a shop in Liaoyang or Beijing: a smithy of your own (income from your smithing), or a stall.
55. Army contracts: the Commission buys 20 blades at a time.
56. Price shocks: the 1619 defeat doubles grain; Beijing grain falls when the canal fleet arrives (autumn).
57. Silver vs copper: prices in wen, salaries in taels (800 wen).

## R6: law, crime, standing
58. Theft at a market stall (a chance to be caught: beaten, or worse).
59. A petition to the magistrate when wronged (a land grab against your family).
60. Repute (名聲) by place: kindness and violence are remembered.
61. Rumours: folk talk about other folk you know (who married, who died, who is in debt).
62. A fight in the teahouse: drunken soldier picks on you.
63. Sworn brotherhood (結拜) at high liking: a sworn brother helps on the road and in trouble.

## R7: office and factions
64. The Hanlin path: top jinshi go to the Hanlin Academy; a slow but high road.
65. Supervising censor: memorials matter more; impeachments.
66. Donglin Academy (首善書院 lectures, 1622): attend, become counted as Donglin.
67. Wei Zhongxian's living shrines (生祠, 1626–27): officials compete to build them; refuse or join.
68. Factions as numbers: Donglin standing vs eunuch party standing; purges read them.
69. Disaster relief: famine in your county; open the granary without permission (Hai Rui-style) or wait.
70. Tax quotas and the 1618–1620 Liaodong levies (遼餉) raised three times: real.
71. Chongzhen's accession (1627/8) purges Wei Zhongxian's party: eunuch-party men fall.

## R8: legacy
72. When you die with a son, play on as your son.
73. Life goals the game recognises: a degree, an office, a family, wealth, a name.
74. The tombstone page, kept in a hall of lives (the last lives you lived).

## R9: travel and land
75. Winter roads: +50% days; snow in the scene.
76. Rain days: travel slower; the teahouse fuller.
77. Hunting in the hills in autumn (furs to sell).
78. Fishing at the Hun River.
79. Night in a roadside temple (a free bed, with stories).

## R10: battles and modes
80. A Last Stand map of Shenyang's walls in 1621.
81. The world's battles use your made weapons and your arms skill.
82. A "witness" camera: when the world fires a historic event at your location, you see it.

## Cut, and why
- Fishing and hunting minigames (R9 77–78): as text verbs only; no new physics.
- Playing as a woman: the world's exams and offices were closed to women; a woman's life is a different game deserving its own design. Not now.
- Real portraits of historical people: not allowed and not needed.
- An economy simulated at the level of every sack of grain: the cost is high and the player can't see it; prices by date and place are enough.
